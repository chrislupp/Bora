# How NeuralFoil works

This page describes the model Bora evaluates. The design is Peter Sharpe's,
and the authoritative account is the NeuralFoil paper
([Sharpe and Hansman, 2025](../references.md#neuralfoil-paper)) together with
the chapter on NeuralFoil in his thesis
([Sharpe, 2024](../references.md#sharpe-thesis)). What follows is a summary
for users of Bora, with the exact transformations as Bora implements them in
`src/neuralfoil.rs`.

## A learned core inside a physical frame

NeuralFoil is a neural network trained to reproduce XFoil
([Drela, 1989](../references.md#xfoil)). The network itself is small and
ordinary. What makes the model work is the structure around it, which builds
known aerodynamics into the calculation so that the network has less to learn
and extrapolates sensibly. Sharpe describes the result as physics-informed
machine learning, while noting that it is not a "PINN" in the usual sense of
a network trained on the residual of a differential equation.

An analysis runs in five steps.

## Encoding

The 23 user inputs become a vector of 25 numbers, the network's input. The
18 shape parameters pass through almost unchanged. The operating point is
transformed:

| Network input | Computed from | Why |
|---------------|---------------|-----|
| 8 upper and 8 lower weights, leading-edge weight | Unchanged | |
| $50\,t_{TE}$ | Trailing-edge thickness | Brings it to the same scale as the weights |
| $\sin 2\alpha$ | Angle of attack | Proportional to lift in simple post-stall models |
| $\cos\alpha$ | Angle of attack | Completes a periodic description of the angle |
| $\sin^2\alpha$ | Angle of attack | Proportional to drag in simple post-stall models |
| $(\ln Re - 12.5)/3.5$ | Reynolds number | Drag follows power laws in $Re$, which are straight lines in $\ln Re$ |
| $(N_\mathrm{crit} - 9)/4.5$ | Critical amplification factor | Centered and scaled |
| $x_{tr,u}$, $x_{tr,l}$ | Forced transition locations | Unchanged |

The scalings center typical inputs near zero with a spread near one, which
helps training. The trigonometric encoding of the angle of attack makes the
model periodic in $\alpha$ by construction: 0° and 360° are the same input.
Taking the logarithm of the Reynolds number, together with the logarithm of
the drag on the output side, means that a straight line in the network's
coordinates is a power law in physical ones, which is what boundary-layer
theory predicts for both laminar and turbulent skin friction.

## The network

The 25 inputs pass through a fully connected network to 198 outputs. Each
hidden layer applies a weight matrix and bias and then the Swish activation,

$$
\sigma(x) = \frac{x}{1 + e^{-x}} .
$$

Swish is smooth everywhere, so the whole model is infinitely differentiable,
which is what makes its gradients useful to an optimizer. It also grows
linearly for large arguments, so far from the training data the network tends
toward a straight line rather than flattening out, which suits the logarithmic
encodings above. The eight [network sizes](../guide/model-sizes.md) differ
only in the number of hidden layers (2 to 6) and their width (48 to 512).

## Symmetry {/* #symmetry */}

Flip an airfoil top to bottom and negate the angle of attack, and the flow is
the mirror image of the original: the lift and moment change sign, the drag
does not, and the upper and lower surfaces trade places. A plain network
would only learn this approximately.

NeuralFoil enforces it exactly. Every analysis evaluates the network twice,
once for the airfoil as given and once for its mirror image at $-\alpha$, maps
the second result back, and averages the two. In terms of the network $f$ and
the mirror operations $M_\mathrm{in}$ and $M_\mathrm{out}$,

$$
z_\mathrm{out} = \tfrac{1}{2}\left[\, f(z_\mathrm{in})
+ M_\mathrm{out}\, f(M_\mathrm{in}\, z_\mathrm{in}) \,\right] .
$$

The average is symmetric by construction, whatever $f$ is. This is why a
symmetric section at zero angle of attack returns exactly zero lift and
moment. Bora evaluates the pair in a single pass over the weights, so the two
evaluations cost less than twice one.

## Decoding

The 198 network outputs are six scalars followed by six boundary-layer
distributions of 32 values each. They are converted to physical quantities as
follows, writing $z$ for the corresponding network output.

| Quantity | Decoding |
|----------|----------|
| Analysis confidence | Logistic function of $z$ after the distance correction below |
| $C_L$ | $z/2$ |
| $C_D$ | $\exp\!\big(2\,(z - 2)\big)$ |
| $C_M$ | $z/20$ |
| Transition locations | $z$ clipped to $[0, 1]$ |
| Momentum thickness $\theta$ | $\dfrac{10^{z} - 0.1}{\lvert u_e/V_\infty\rvert\; Re}$ |
| Shape factor $H$ | $2.6\, e^{z}$ |
| Edge velocity $u_e/V_\infty$ | $z$ |

The exponential guarantees a positive drag coefficient. The momentum
thickness is learned through a Reynolds number based on it, $Re_\theta$, and
the shape factor relative to its laminar flat-plate value of 2.6; in both
cases the network predicts a quantity that varies far less across the
training data than the raw one.

## Confidence {/* #confidence */}

The first network output is a logit: the logarithm of the odds that XFoil
would converge for this case. Before it is turned into a probability, a term
is subtracted that grows with the distance of the query from the training
data,

$$
\text{confidence} = \operatorname{logistic}\!\left(
z_0 - \frac{(z_\mathrm{in} - \mu)^\mathsf{T}\, S^{-1}\, (z_\mathrm{in} - \mu)}{2 N}
\right), \qquad N = 25 ,
$$

where $\mu$ and $S$ are the mean and covariance of the training inputs in the
encoded space. The numerator is a squared Mahalanobis distance: a distance
measured in units of the data's own spread along each direction.

Without this term nothing would force the confidence down far from the data,
since a network's raw output can drift anywhere. With it, the confidence is
guaranteed to tend to zero in every direction, because the quadratic
eventually dominates the roughly linear growth of the logit. For a query
drawn from the training distribution the term averages to 0.5, a small
correction. The same term was present during training, so it does not distort
the model inside the data.

Bora stores $\mu$ and $S^{-1}$ in `scaled_input_distribution.h5` and applies
the correction to both of the mirrored evaluations before they are averaged.

## Training

Bora does not train anything; it uses NeuralFoil's published weights. For
context, the paper reports the following.

- **Data.** 7.9 million XFoil cases on synthetic airfoils, each made by
  blending three airfoils from a database of about 2,000, scaling the
  thickness and perturbing the Kulfan parameters. Angle of attack, Reynolds
  number, $N_\mathrm{crit}$ and forced transition were drawn at random. All
  cases are incompressible.
- **Convergence.** 56% of the cases converged in XFoil. The rest were not
  used to train the physical outputs, but whether a case converged is exactly
  the label the confidence output learns.
- **Split.** 95% of the data trained the networks; 5% was held back to measure
  the accuracies quoted in [Choosing a network size](../guide/model-sizes.md).
- **Loss.** A Huber loss on the encoded outputs, weighted most heavily toward
  drag. Sharpe notes that drag accuracy improved when the boundary-layer
  outputs were given some weight too, rather than training on drag alone.

## What Bora adds

Bora implements the five steps above so that they reproduce Python NeuralFoil
0.3.3, and it differentiates them. The forward-mode path carries a 23-column
Jacobian through every layer alongside the values. The reverse-mode path
propagates one adjoint per scalar output backward through the layers. Both
are written by hand against the same kernels as the value computation; no
automatic-differentiation library is involved. See
[Gradients](../guide/gradients.md).
