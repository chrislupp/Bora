# Airfoil optimization

`examples/optimize_airfoil.rs` reshapes a NACA 0012 to minimize drag at a
lift coefficient of 0.8 and a Reynolds number of 10⁶. It is a small, complete
gradient-based design loop with no dependencies beyond Bora.

```bash
cargo run --release --example optimize_airfoil
```

## The problem

| Role | Quantity |
|------|----------|
| Minimize | $C_D$ |
| Subject to | $C_L = 0.8$ |
| | analysis confidence ≥ 0.95 |
| Design variables | 16 surface weights, the leading-edge weight, and the angle of attack |
| Fixed | Reynolds number 10⁶, trailing-edge thickness, free transition |

Both constraints are handled as quadratic penalties, so the problem becomes
the unconstrained minimization of

$$
J = C_D + w_L\,(C_L - 0.8)^2 + w_c\,\max(0,\; 0.95 - c)^2 ,
$$

with $c$ the analysis confidence, $w_L = 1$ and $w_c = 0.1$. The 18 design
variables are updated with Adam, a first-order method that needs only $J$ and
its gradient.

This is a deliberately simple formulation. The case study in the NeuralFoil
paper, the airfoil of the Daedalus human-powered aircraft, is a better model
for real work: it minimizes drag averaged over six lift coefficients and
constrains the pitching moment, the thickness at two spar stations and the
trailing-edge angle ([Sharpe and Hansman, 2025](../references.md#neuralfoil-paper)).

## Objective and gradient

Each iteration makes one call. The objective uses only scalar outputs, so the
reverse-mode scalar path is enough, and for NeuralFoil's xlarge network it is about
4.5 times faster than `analyze_with_gradients`.

```rust title="examples/optimize_airfoil.rs (abridged)"
let result = model.analyze_scalars_with_gradients(&input);
let aero = result.output;

let cl_error = aero.cl - CL_TARGET;
let confidence_deficit = (CONFIDENCE_FLOOR - aero.analysis_confidence).max(0.0);

let value = aero.cd
    + CL_PENALTY_WEIGHT * cl_error * cl_error
    + CONFIDENCE_PENALTY_WEIGHT * confidence_deficit * confidence_deficit;

let g = &result.gradient;
let gradient: [f64; NUM_DESIGN_VARS] = std::array::from_fn(|i| {
    // Surface and leading-edge weights map one to one; the last entry is alpha.
    let k = design_to_input_index(i);
    g.cd[k] + 2.0 * CL_PENALTY_WEIGHT * cl_error * g.cl[k]
        - 2.0 * CONFIDENCE_PENALTY_WEIGHT * confidence_deficit * g.analysis_confidence[k]
});
```

The gradient of $J$ is the chain rule applied to the three terms, each a row
of the returned gradient. Nothing is differenced.

The lift target is met within the first 100 iterations. From there the drag
at that lift falls from 0.0061 to 0.0052, and the lift-to-drag ratio ends at
155. The whole run is 500 gradient evaluations, about 36 ms of analysis time
on an Apple M1 Pro.

The confidence drifts down from 0.97 to just under the 0.95 floor in the last
iterations: the optimizer is leaning on the constraint, and the penalty is
holding it there. That is the constraint doing its job. It is also a signal
to stop, or to confirm the design with XFoil, rather than to keep iterating.

## What to take from it

**Penalize or constrain the confidence.** It keeps the search where the model
is trustworthy and, as a side effect, keeps the shape reasonable. See
[Analysis confidence](../guide/confidence.md).

**Make the angle of attack a design variable.** Letting the optimizer trim
the angle alongside the shape makes a lift target easy to hold.

**Use a real optimizer for real problems.** Adam with penalties is enough to
show the gradients working. A constrained method such as SQP or an
interior-point code, fed the same gradients, will converge in far fewer
iterations and satisfy constraints exactly. The
[Philote discipline](../guide/philote.md) connects Bora to frameworks that
provide them.

**Regularize the shape on harder problems.** The NeuralFoil paper recommends
two constraints as good practice: positive thickness everywhere, and a limit
on the roughness of the shape measured by the second differences of
neighboring Kulfan weights, held to a few times its value for the starting
airfoil ([Sharpe and Hansman, 2025](../references.md#neuralfoil-paper)).
Neither was needed here.

**Confirm the result.** The optimized section is a prediction of a surrogate
of XFoil. Check it with XFoil or a higher-fidelity method before building
anything.

The example writes the final shape to `optimized_airfoil.dat` in Selig format
through `generate_kulfan_coordinates`.
